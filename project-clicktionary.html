Clicktionary

Chrome extension for language learners · 4-person Product Space team · I was the only engineer · Live on the Chrome Web Store

HTML/CSS Supabase Chrome Extension Product Management

Clicktionary is a Chrome extension for language learners, where you highlight a word on any webpage and a translation pops up right next to it, in any of 10 languages. You can save the word to your word bank and practice it later with flashcards and multiple choice, and it's live on the Chrome Web Store now!

The Team

We built Clicktionary as a four-person team in Product Space: Nicole, Sophia, Roslyn, and me, and we made the product decisions together, like which features mattered and how it should look. I was the only engineer, so I built the whole extension and its practice site.

The Problem

We started from our own experience as college students learning Spanish. Reading real content is one of the best ways to learn a language, but every unfamiliar word means switching tabs, pasting it into a translator, and finding your place again, and you've usually forgotten the word by the next day.

Research

The American Council on the Teaching of Foreign Languages says the key to reaching an advanced level is using the language as much as possible, and time is one of the biggest things holding adult learners back, and college students don't have much of it. Our own user research added three things: learners got more out of dictionaries than translators, it took about three lookups before a word stuck, and gamified apps weren't an efficient way to learn.

We also looked at what people were already using, and each option had a gap. SpanishDict gives the most complete information, with multiple definitions, example sentences, and conjugations, but it breaks your reading flow and only covers Spanish. Google Translate is fast but gives you less about each word, so it sticks less. Quizlet is the best for retention because you practice, but setting up your own sets is tedious. Nobody combined a quick lookup on the page with practice afterward, so that became our product!

From Pitch to Launch

Our pitch had four steps: click a word, translate it, see a definition with the option to save it, and practice. In the pitch, practice meant sending your saved words to Quizlet, but in the version that shipped I built our own practice site instead, with flashcards, multiple choice, and a mastery score for each word.

How It Works

When you highlight text, a small tooltip shows the translation from Lingva Translate, an open-source front end for Google Translate, with MyMemory as a backup. From there you can save the word in one click, or type your own definition if the lookup gets it wrong.

  • The extension asks several Lingva servers at once and uses whichever answers first, with a 2-second timeout, so one slow server doesn't hold up the tooltip.
  • When a page loads, it translates the 10 longest words on the page ahead of time. Longer words tend to be rarer, so they're the ones you're most likely to highlight, and they show up instantly when you do.
  • Words you've already looked up are cached, so looking them up again doesn't need the network.
  • If you're signed in, saved words sync to Supabase. If you're not, they stay in the browser, so the extension works without an account or a connection.
  • A companion site on GitHub Pages holds your full word bank and the practice tools: flashcards, multiple choice, and a mastery score from 0 to 5 for each word.
  • A small script passes your login from the extension to the practice site, so you only sign in once.

Technical Highlights

Manifest V3 Architecture

Built on Manifest V3, the current Chrome extension standard. A background service worker handles sign-in, syncing, and the word bank.

Google OAuth

Sign-in uses chrome.identity.launchWebAuthFlow. I pinned the extension's ID with an RSA key in the manifest so the OAuth redirect URL stays the same.

Duplicate Detection & Fallbacks

The extension skips words you've already saved, lets you write your own definition if a translation fails, and remembers whether you turned it off.

10-Language Support

Supports Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Arabic, and Russian. Before showing a tooltip, it checks the characters you highlighted to see if they could belong to the language you're learning.
